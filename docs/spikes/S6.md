# S6: SQLite write path (sidecar commits, a second process)

Status: done 2026-11-13 on the workstation (4 CPUs). Pass: edit batches of
up to 100 operations commit in under 35 ms p95 on a sidecar holding a 1M
polygon layer; another process's commits are detected within about 10 ms.

## Setup

- Sidecar: `server/src/compass_studio/annot/sidecar.py`. COMPASS schema plus
  viewer tables `cs_state` (revision), `cs_oplog` (ops and inverse ops per
  batch), `cs_forks` (provenance). WAL, `busy_timeout` 5 s. Batches run in
  `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, check `base_revision`, apply, bump the revision and
  append to the log in one transaction.
- Data: the 1M-cell layer forked from the synthetic store (R-tree trigger per
  row). Harness: `spikes/S6/bench.py`. SQLite 3.45.
- Batches of 1, 10, 100 and 1,000 operations of each kind (30 repetitions,
  6 for 1,000), synchronous NORMAL and FULL; undo of the last 20 batches.

## Results

Fork of 1,000,000 polygons: 8.4 s, 548 MB sidecar (200 regions: 0.45 s).

Commit time, synchronous NORMAL, p50 / p95 (ms):

| Operation | 1 | 10 | 100 | 1,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| create | 0.15 / 0.38 | 0.7 / 2.0 | 6.6 / 19 | 92 / 96 |
| update_geometry | 0.21 / 0.31 | 1.2 / 16 | 21 / 30 | 194 / 203 |
| move | 0.07 / 0.10 | 0.3 / 2.2 | 6.0 / 18 | 47 / 48 |
| update_attributes | 0.12 / 0.15 | 0.6 / 0.7 | 5.0 / 27 | 67 / 76 |
| delete | 0.19 / 0.35 | 1.2 / 19 | 24 / 32 | 222 / 227 |
| mixed | 0.23 / 0.32 | 1.0 / 19 | 21 / 33 | 139 / 141 |

- synchronous FULL adds about 3 ms per commit (an fsync); the large batches
  are unchanged. Undo (replaying inverses, including 1,000-op batches):
  p95 113 ms.
- The p95 jumps at 10 to 100 ops are WAL checkpoints landing in a commit.
- Delete and update_geometry are the costly kinds: they read the old row to
  record the inverse (WKB to coordinates) and touch the R-tree.

Second process (an external writer committing 10 creates every 100 ms for
5 s, this process polling `PRAGMA data_version` every 10 ms):

- 49 of 49 external commits detected; lag p50 6 ms, p95 10 ms (bounded by
  the poll period).
- Bbox queries in the reading process stayed at 0.26 ms p95 during the
  writes (WAL: readers do not block on the writer). Writer commit p95 3.6 ms.

Two writers committing as fast as they can for 5 s: 4,517 commits, 1,063
refused as conflicts (stale `base_revision`, as intended), no busy errors;
commit p95 4.2 ms.

## Implications

- Interactive edits (1 to 100 operations) fit the 100 ms round trip with
  room for HTTP and rendering (S5 measured 20 to 65 ms end to end).
- Bulk batches of about 1,000 operations take 50 to 230 ms: fine for
  scripted or bulk actions, worth a progress indicator in the UI.
- Forking a 1M-object layer takes 8 to 9 s and doubles its storage. If that
  is too slow in practice, a copy-on-write overlay (fork = metadata; copy an
  object only when it is first edited; tombstones for deletes) is the
  alternative; not needed for v1 on this evidence.
- External changes are detectable cheaply with `PRAGMA data_version`
  polling, which can drive the server-sent events of D2.

## Not tested

- Slow disks or network file systems (fsync cost).
- Sidecars much larger than one forked layer; long operation logs.
- Crash recovery mid-batch (relied on SQLite's atomic commit).
